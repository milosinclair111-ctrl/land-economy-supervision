# Question

- Question: Has remote work made London more dominant?
- Theory or mechanism: Krugman's (1991) core–periphery model, in the Fujita–Krugman–Venables form. Remote work acts on it in two opposite ways: it lowers the cost of trading services (T), which strengthens concentration in the biggest market, and it lets people earn in one region and spend in another. I add the second as θ, the share of each region's wages spent in the other region, which weakens concentration.
- Expected pattern: If the fall in T dominates, jobs that can be done remotely should become more concentrated in London relative to jobs that cannot. If θ dominates, they should not, and more London pay should flow to people living outside London. The model says each 10 points of θ needs a 12–16% fall in T to offset it.
- Scope: Great Britain, NUTS1 regions, 2015–2024 (pay to 2025), comparing 2015–19 with 2022–24 and leaving out 2020–21.
