## Question

Has remote work made London more dominant?

## Findings

- London's share of Britain's remote-able jobs rose from 30.9% (2015–19) to 33.2% (2022–24); its share of in-person jobs moved from 13.5% to 13.9%.
- Difference-in-differences across 59 industries: remote-able jobs became about 6.0% more concentrated in London than in-person jobs (p = 0.056). Allowing pre-2020 trends: +11.1% (p = 0.062). Placebo starting in 2017: −4.6%, the opposite direction.
- Boundaries: London within London + South East + East +6.7% (p = 0.005); the wider region against GB -1.0% (p = 0.424).
- The share of London workplace pay going to non-residents rose from 26.8% to 29.8%.

## Interpretation and limitations

Yes, a little, but mostly at the expense of its neighbours. Remote-able jobs moved towards London, so the fall in trade costs outweighed the spending leak, but the gain came from London's own commuter belt, so the wider London region did not pull away from the rest of Britain. The main test is borderline rather than conclusive. Limits: only three post-2020 years; industries rather than jobs; the tech boom and the Elizabeth line happened at the same time.
